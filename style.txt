# Scientific Figure Style

Apply all relevant requirements below. Explicit task requirements override defaults.

## Content organization

Let the method determine the modules and relationships:

- Explain the central mechanism and preserve its actual inputs and outputs.
- Follow the method's reading order and use spatial regions to show module hierarchy.
- Connect branches and loops according to their real direction and meaning.
- Keep text near its object and reserve space for labels and unobstructed connections.

### Abstraction

Choose details according to the question the figure should answer:

- Summarize standard modules as named blocks; expand the central mechanism as needed.
- Express actions through visual structure and use concise keywords inside modules.
  - Preserve complete sentences when needed, such as example queries or sample text.
  - Give these examples dedicated areas; avoid filling boxes with paragraphs from the paper.
- Keep only mathematical relationships needed to explain the mechanism.
  - Match symbols to the objects shown; leave derivations to the paper.
  - Preserve meaning when shortening labels or simplifying structure.
- Repeat elements only for a semantic purpose, such as successive iterations.
  - Merge redundant module labels.
  - Use justified omissions for repetitive structures while preserving their relationships.

### Composition

Choose the layout from the method; explicit composition requirements take priority:

- Make both the overall reading path and local details easy to follow.
  - A sequence may use horizontal flow; feedback may use a loop. These are examples.
  - Align parallel branches at corresponding stages and identify inputs at each merge.
- Keep major modules in the overview; expand important internal mechanisms when useful.
  - Connect each detail view to its source module.
  - Reuse the module's name and color in its detail view.
- Use spacing and alignment to reveal hierarchy and leave connections room.
- Allocate area by explanatory importance; the central mechanism may need more space.

### Canvas use

Keep the composition full, balanced, and spacious enough to read:

- Place structural detail views and concise legends near their objects, within the composition.
- Use roughly even spacing among peers and coordinate neighboring regions' density.
  - Relieve crowding and rearrange or tighten unusually empty regions.
  - Allow modest density differences when justified by the content.
- Fit the canvas to the content while preserving mechanisms and reading space.
- Respect any requested aspect ratio; rearrange content rather than distort proportions.

## Connections

Give each line style a stable meaning and land endpoints on their intended objects.

### Routing

Make every major module's general flow clear:

- Choose directions from the mechanism and reading order.
  - Different major modules may have different directions.
  - Keep meaningful local turns and feedback paths.
- Minimize bends by adjusting node positions and connection ports.
  - Prefer straight lines for relationships that can be connected clearly.
  - Remove short staircase detours that only compensate for slight endpoint misalignment.
- Keep necessary offsets and turns clear.
  - A straight diagonal is suitable when endpoints must remain offset.
  - Keep bends for obstacles, branches, or feedback; judge purpose at the actual drawing scale.
- Share segments or arrowheads only for same-direction arrows with clear, compatible meanings.
  - Preserve identifiable origins, destinations, branch points, and directions.
  - Shared lines must not falsely imply that data have merged.

### Semantics

Make the relationship represented by every connection clear:

- Distinguish detail-view links from data-flow arrows.
- Distinguish auxiliary relations through line styles or short labels; color is supplemental.
  - Solid forward flow and dashed updates are examples, not fixed assignments.
  - Derive meanings from the method; avoid ambiguous reuse of the same line style.
- Distinguish crossings from junctions; mark junctions only at real connections.
- Put operators at their actual operation and make their inputs clear.
  - Use a plus sign for addition when appropriate.
  - Label concatenation with its actual meaning.

## Visual representations

Use structural diagrams for internal organization and icons to identify objects or functions.

### Shapes

Choose representations according to their technical meaning:

- Use icons where they summarize an object, such as a user or tool.
  - Avoid decorating every step with an icon.
  - Show structure through shapes, such as grids for matrices or stacks for tensor dimensions.
- Preserve depth where it explains spatial or multidimensional relationships.
  - Convolution kernels, multidimensional parameters, and 3D operators may remain volumetric.
  - Flat styling applies to icons; retain technical 3D structures and required photographs.
- Represent equivalent structures consistently; changes in dimensionality need a content reason.
  - Logical steps may use 2D blocks; volume data may use 3D stacks.
  - Keep related 3D viewpoints consistent and prevent shading from hiding important outlines.
- Give technical shapes and panel boundaries stable meanings.
  - Cylinders may denote storage; grids may denote matrices; operations remain process nodes.
  - Dashed panels may denote logical stages when that meaning fits the method.

Use square corners for rectangular panels and cards, and sharp corners for bent lines.

Natural circles and smooth, continuous curved arrows are allowed where appropriate.

### Icons

Use lineal color or restrained flat icons, or a coherent combination:

- Lineal color uses clear outlines and simple colored fills.
- Restrained flat uses simple color shapes and limited shading, with outlines as needed.
- Coordinate outline strength, color layers, and depth across every icon, including mixed styles.
- Preserve meaningful state marks, such as a lock that denotes frozen parameters.

Check each icon against the chosen visual language:

- Avoid photorealistic rendering, strong material highlights, and toy-like 3D icons.
- Preserve technical 3D diagrams and real photographs required by the content.
- Choose each icon's form from its meaning and apply the overall palette and font rules.
- Correct individual deviations and recheck the consistency of the whole figure.

### Color

Use rich pastel colors with a soft base, substantial color, and clear layers.

Color temperature is unrestricted. Choose exact colors to suit the figure:

- Give the page background, local panels, and internal elements distinct color roles.
  - Keep backgrounds softer than their contents and retain enough foreground color.
  - Keep text and connections clear against panel fills.
- Use different hues for icons and their local backgrounds.
- Use different hues for arrows and text; arrow labels follow the text palette.
- Apply these relationships to structural diagrams as well as icons.

Keep color meanings stable across regions:

- Match colors for the same object type or state; differences need a semantic reason.
- Distinguish states such as trainable and frozen modules when relevant.
  - Assign warm or cool colors according to this figure's meaning.
  - Use labels to support state recognition.
- Concentrate stronger accents on important mechanisms or outputs.
  - Avoid making every module compete equally for attention.
  - Keep only the legend information needed to explain colors.
- Support key categories with labels or shapes; preserve main relationships in grayscale.

## Typography

Use the method's terminology and keep text readable at the final display size:

- Use Times New Roman for English.
- Use STSong for Chinese, with NSimSun as the fallback.
- Keep typography consistent for equivalent text.
- Keep ordinary text at regular weight; establish hierarchy through size and position first.

### Emphasis

Choose emphasis according to the actual content and visual hierarchy:

- Use bold selectively for justified priorities, such as a central mechanism label.
- Avoid uniformly bold figures, paragraphs, or labels.
- Preserve bold mathematical symbols when the weight carries meaning.
- Follow explicit emphasis requests; otherwise choose emphasis from the actual composition.

### Placement and notation

Keep text accurate, complete, and close to its object:

- Preserve mathematical symbols, subscripts, and operation relationships.
  - Distinguish similar-looking characters that represent different variables.
  - Keep notation consistent with the method.
- Use short labels; place longer explanations in the external caption or paper text.
- Rearrange crowded areas instead of repeatedly shrinking text.
- Keep paper captions outside the figure; retain necessary module names and subfigure markers.

## Visual explanation

Explain mechanisms through the drawing:

- Use shapes, connections, operators, or local detail views to show how a component works.
- Replace explanatory text patches with a visual representation of the intended relationship.
- Do not append parenthetical explanations or descriptive clauses to module names.
- Keep technical names and notation accurate; parentheses within a required name remain valid.

Keep explanatory prose outside the figure:

- Do not add an overall figure title, headline, subtitle, summary, or caption-like paragraph.
- Reserve figure titles and longer explanations for the external caption or paper text.
- Keep only short object labels, mathematical notation, data ticks, and concise legend keys.
- Retain source text when it is itself the depicted data, such as an input query or sample.

A label identifies an object. Use visual structure to explain its role or internal mechanism.

If a detail needs explanation, improve the diagram before adding words.

## Data displays

Distinguish measured data from conceptual illustrations before choosing the visual form:

- Preserve real values and series assignments; include necessary axes, units, and ranges.
- Identify conceptual illustrations as such; do not invent experimental results.
- Choose charts for the comparison, such as bars for categories or lines for continuous changes.
- Make graphical sizes correspond to values.

Keep scales readable and reading aids unobtrusive:

- Order color scales by magnitude or sign and retain necessary ticks.
- Use comparable scales for the same metric across charts and state each chart's range.
- Keep grids beneath the data and visually weaker than it.
- Keep legends away from important points; name series nearby when clear.

Apply rich pastel styling to data regions while preserving accurate readings.

## Reference images

Use a reference image only when explicitly supplied for this task:

- Borrow visual treatment while deriving content and connections from the method.
- Choose component count and layout freely to explain the method.
- Do not infer a need to preserve the reference layout or reproduce it pixel for pixel.
- Explicit style requirements take priority over conflicting reference characteristics.

## Final image checks

Check the actual image against the method and every applicable requirement.

### Scientific accuracy

Verify content before accepting appearance:

- Check required modules and connections for omissions, additions, or reversed direction.
- Keep illustrative examples consistent with the method.
- Check spelling, label completeness, mathematical symbols, and meaningful font weights.
- For real data, check every value, tick, and series assignment; keep legends clear of key data.

### Readability

Inspect the whole figure and its smallest labels at the intended display size:

- Check text hierarchy, font appearance, regular body weight, and justified emphasis.
- Remove overall titles and caption-like prose; keep captions outside the image.
- Check every major module's path, routing, crossings, and obstructions.
  - Remove unnecessary short staircase turns; verify local turns and feedback paths.
  - Check shared segments and arrowheads for clear origins, destinations, and branch meanings.
- Check balanced density, roughly even spacing, and modest local density differences.

### Text discipline

Inspect every text item before accepting the figure:

- Remove parenthetical explanations and prose attached to module names as visual fixes.
- Express their technical meaning through structure, connections, or local detail views.
- Remove overall titles, subtitles, narrative callouts, and summary paragraphs.
- Keep necessary identifiers and depicted source data; move other prose to the external caption.

### Visual consistency

Inspect objects individually and in context:

- Check square rectangular corners and sharp bends, with the stated circle and curve exceptions.
- Check rich pastel layering, icon/background hue differences, and arrow/text hue differences.
- Check each icon's style and state meaning; preserve structural diagrams' technical information.
- Remove blurred outlines, distorted objects, accidental editing grids, and watermarks.

### Revisions

Correct identified defects while retaining accurate content and effective design:

1. Identify the region, the defect, and the intended result.
2. Change what resolves the defect while preserving all unaffected requirements.
3. Preserve module meanings and connections; verify meaning before shortening labels.
4. Base structural changes on the method or explicit task requirements.
5. Recheck the changed region and related content for omissions or new changes.
6. Recheck all applicable requirements after every revision and retain unresolved issues.
7. Accept only when method accuracy, readability, and all applicable requirements pass.

Keep the current design when several designs satisfy all requirements.

Avoid repeated redraws based only on personal preference.
